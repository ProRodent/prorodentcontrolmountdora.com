When exclusion is the right first step.

Some calls come in before any animal has actually gotten inside. That usually means a homeowner noticed a gap during a home inspection, saw a rat working at a vent screen from the outside, or wants prevention work done ahead of a planned renovation that will disturb the roofline. In these cases exclusion can be the first and only step needed, since there is nothing to remove yet, only a path to close before it gets used. Tell us on the call whether you have actually seen activity inside the house or are calling about something you noticed from the outside, since that changes how the visit is planned.

Why the material matters.

A gap sealed with standard caulk or foam looks finished but does not stay that way, since both mice and rats can chew through soft material within days if the opening still smells like a route they have used before. Exclusion work uses steel wool packed into small gaps, galvanized hardware cloth over larger openings like vents, and metal flashing at rooflines and chimney chases, matched to the size and location of the specific opening found during inspection.

How long a typical job takes.

A single entry point at a vent or utility line can often be sealed in under an hour once it has been identified. A whole-house exclusion job covering multiple gaps, a roofline, and a garage door takes longer, usually planned as a half-day visit depending on how many separate points were found during inspection. We give a realistic estimate of the time involved before starting so there is no surprise about how long the technician will be on site.

Common entry points in Mount Dora homes.

Older homes near the Historic Downtown District often have gaps around original plumbing chases, gaps where a later addition meets the original roofline, and worn weatherstripping under garage doors. Newer construction farther from downtown tends to have fewer of these but can still have gaps around utility penetrations, dryer vents, and soffit vents where a screen has rusted or come loose over time.

Every house is different, so exclusion work always follows Rodent Inspection and Estimates or an active removal job where entry points were already identified, rather than being done as a guess at what might be a problem.

Rooflines and tree canopy.

Mount Dora's mature oak canopy, part of what makes neighborhoods near the lake and Palm Island Park so shaded and appealing, also gives roof rats an easy bridge onto a house. Exclusion work at a roofline is often paired with a recommendation to trim back any limb within a few feet of the structure, since sealing a gap does little good if a new one opens up nearby from continued gnawing at a fresh access point.

Doors, vents and utility penetrations.

Garage door weatherstripping wears down over time and is one of the more overlooked entry points, since a worn strip can leave a gap easily wide enough for a mouse along the full width of the door. Dryer vents, exterior spigot penetrations, and gas or electrical lines passing through a wall are checked and sealed with material that will not degrade the way basic caulk does over a Florida summer.

A sealed gap only holds if the material can survive what chews at it.

What exclusion does not include.

Sealing closes the path in, but it does not remove anything already inside. If activity is active at the time of the visit, we combine sealing with Rodent Trapping and Removal so the animals already in the space are cleared out rather than sealed in, which would create a worse problem than the one you started with.

Checking the work holds.

A follow-up visit confirms sealed points are intact and checks for any new gnawing nearby that would suggest an animal is still trying to get back in through the same general area. Materials are chosen to hold up to routine gnawing pressure, but confirming the work in person is still part of every job.

Exclusion on commercial buildings.

Shops and restaurants near downtown often have loading dock doors, utility chases, and shared walls with a neighboring business, any of which can become an entry point that a homeowner-style fix would not address. Commercial Rodent Control folds exclusion work into a broader monitoring plan, since a business open to the public needs documentation of what was sealed and when, not just a one-time repair.

What homeowners can check between visits.

A quick walk around the outside of the house after any storm is worth doing, since heavy wind and rain can knock loose a screen or shift a piece of flashing that was previously secure. Anything that looks different from the last visit, a gap that seems wider or a new pile of debris near the foundation, is worth mentioning on the next call even if nothing has been seen moving yet.

Questions about rodent exclusion and entry point sealing

Why not just use caulk?

Standard caulk and foam are soft enough that mice and rats can chew back through them within days, especially at a gap they have already used.

Will sealing trap animals inside?

Not when it is done correctly. If activity is active, sealing is paired with trapping so animals already inside are removed rather than sealed in.

Do I need to trim my trees?

If a limb comes within a few feet of the roof, trimming it back removes an easy bridge for roof rats and is one of the most effective steps a homeowner can take.

How long does exclusion work last?

The materials used are chosen to resist gnawing well beyond what basic caulk or foam would, and a follow-up visit checks that the work is holding.

Does exclusion cover the whole house?

It covers the entry points identified during inspection or an active removal job, which is why we start with a walkthrough rather than sealing at random.
