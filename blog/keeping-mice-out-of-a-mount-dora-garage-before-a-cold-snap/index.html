Keeping Mice Out of a Garage Before a Cold Snap

Central Florida cold snaps are short but real, and a garage that has not been checked in a while is exactly the kind of shelter a mouse looks for during one.

Why a cold snap changes the calculation.

A sudden overnight temperature drop, even a short one, triggers the same shelter-seeking instinct in mice that a longer, colder season does farther north. A forecasted cold snap in Mount Dora, even one lasting only a few days, is enough to send nearby mice looking for the nearest warm, enclosed space, and a garage often fits that description better than almost anywhere else on a property.

This pattern repeats every winter regardless of how mild the season is overall, since the trigger is the relative drop in temperature compared to what came before, not an absolute cold threshold. A string of 70-degree days followed by a sudden overnight drop into the 40s produces the same shelter-seeking response as a longer, steadier cold stretch would.

Why garages specifically.

A garage used mainly for storage rather than daily parking is often the least disturbed space in a house, and that quiet makes it especially appealing when a mouse is actively searching for undisturbed shelter. Cardboard boxes, stored fabric, and insulation in an attached garage's ceiling all provide nesting material, while a worn door seal provides the entry point.

Checking the door seal first.

The bottom seal on a garage door wears down over time, and a worn strip can leave a gap running the width of the door, more than enough for a mouse to use anywhere along that length. Checking this from outside during daylight, looking for visible light coming through, is a two-minute check worth doing before any forecasted cold snap.

Storage habits that help.

Switching from cardboard boxes to sealed plastic storage bins removes one of the more common nesting material sources in a garage, and it has the added benefit of protecting stored items from any moisture that gets in during a storm. Keeping stored items a few inches off the floor and away from walls also makes it easier to spot early signs of activity during a routine check.

Vents and utility penetrations.

Dryer vents, exterior spigot penetrations, and any conduit running from the garage to an outdoor unit are worth a quick visual check for gaps or damaged screening. Rodent Exclusion and Entry Point Sealing addresses these professionally with materials that outlast basic caulk, but a homeowner's own visual check can catch an obvious problem before scheduling a visit.

When an inspection makes sense before trouble starts.

A homeowner who has never had a garage rodent problem before, but wants to know exactly where the property's weak points are, benefits from Rodent Inspection and Estimates as a preventive step rather than waiting for an active problem to force the issue. This is especially worth considering for a garage that has not been thoroughly checked in several years, since gaps develop gradually and are easy to miss without a systematic walkthrough.

Older versus newer garages.

A detached garage built before more recent code requirements often has fewer sealed utility penetrations and a simpler, sometimes less weathertight door than a garage built in the last decade. This does not mean newer garages are automatically exempt, only that older structures tend to need a closer look at more potential entry points during a pre-winter check.

What a professional exclusion visit adds.

A homeowner's own visual check catches the more obvious gaps. A professional visit using the same systematic approach applied to the rest of a house often finds smaller openings around conduit, foundation seams, or an aging weep hole that would otherwise go unnoticed until a mouse actually finds and uses it. Rodent Exclusion and Entry Point Sealing closes whatever is found with materials built to outlast a season of gnawing pressure.

Garages shared between multiple units.

Some Mount Dora properties, particularly older ones near downtown, have a garage or storage structure shared between more than one residence. A shared structure with a gap benefits every unit using it, and coordinating a single sealing visit for the shared space is more efficient than each resident addressing it separately without knowing the others are dealing with the same underlying issue.

What to do if you already see signs.

Droppings, gnawed boxes, or a scratching sound from the garage before a cold snap even fully arrives means acting now rather than waiting for the temperature to actually drop. Mouse Control covers the trapping and sealing work needed to clear an active problem before it has a chance to establish itself further during the coldest days.

A garage attached to the house needs extra attention.

An attached garage shares a wall or ceiling cavity with living space, which means a mouse that settles into the garage during a cold snap has a shorter path to the rest of the house than one in a detached structure. This is part of why we treat attached garages with the same seriousness given to the interior of a house itself.

A quick pre-winter checklist.

Setting aside twenty minutes before the first forecasted cold snap of the season covers most of what matters. Walk the garage perimeter, check the door seal, glance at vent screens, and look over stored boxes for any sign of previous activity, all without requiring any special tools or expertise. Anything that looks questionable is worth a follow-up call rather than waiting to see if it becomes an obvious problem.

Preparing every year, not just once.

Central Florida sees multiple cold snaps across a typical winter, not just one, so a single check in November does not cover the whole season. A brief recheck before each forecasted cold snap, or at minimum monthly through the cooler months, keeps a garage from becoming an easy target repeatedly through the same winter.

Common questions

How short can a cold snap be and still cause this?

Even a few days of noticeably lower overnight temperatures is enough to shift mouse behavior toward seeking shelter, regardless of how quickly the weather warms back up afterward.

Is a detached garage safer than an attached one?

A detached garage keeps activity somewhat farther from living space, but it is not immune, and any garage with a worn door seal or unsealed vent is at real risk regardless of attachment.

Do plastic bins really make a difference?

Yes, sealed plastic storage removes a major source of nesting material that cardboard readily provides, and it also protects belongings from any moisture in the space.

Should I check my garage before every cold snap?

A brief recheck before each forecasted cold snap, or at least monthly during the cooler months, is a reasonable habit for most Mount Dora properties.

What is the fastest thing I can check right now?

The garage door's bottom seal, checked from outside for visible daylight, is the single fastest and most useful check to start with.

Do apartment or condo garages need the same attention?

Yes, a shared parking garage or storage structure benefits from the same seal and vent checks, ideally coordinated through the property management company.
