Scratching in the Attic at Night: Mice or Rats?

The sound alone can tell you a surprising amount about what is actually living in your attic, before a technician ever needs to confirm it in person.

Why the sound matters more than people think.

Most homeowners describe attic noise the same general way when they call, something moving around at night, but the specific character of that sound actually carries useful information. Light, quick scurrying that seems to move across a wide area points toward mice, which are smaller and lighter on their feet. Heavier thumping, or a sound that seems to come from one concentrated spot rather than moving around, points more toward a rat, whose greater body weight produces a noticeably different sound against ceiling drywall or attic decking.

Timing tells its own story.

Both mice and rats are nocturnal, so activity at night rather than during the day is expected either way and does not by itself distinguish between them. What matters more is consistency. A sound that happens at roughly the same time every night, often shortly after the house goes quiet, suggests an established nest with a routine, while sporadic, unpredictable noise sometimes points to an animal still exploring rather than settled in.

What the droppings tell you.

If you can safely see into the attic without disturbing anything, droppings are one of the clearest indicators available. Mouse droppings are small, roughly the size of a grain of rice, and tend to be scattered widely across an area a mouse has explored. Rat droppings are noticeably larger, closer to the size of a raisin or an olive pit depending on the species, and tend to cluster more heavily near a nest or a frequently used travel route.

Roof rats and Mount Dora's tree canopy.

Roof rats are the more likely candidate in a lot of Mount Dora neighborhoods specifically, given how much of the city sits under mature oak canopy. A roof rat is a strong climber that uses tree limbs, power lines, and vine growth to reach a roofline before working down through a gap at the eave or a damaged vent screen. If your property has branches touching or close to the roof, that raises the odds of a roof rat over a house mouse considerably.

Why guessing wrong wastes time.

Setting store-bought mouse traps in an attic that actually has a rat problem rarely produces results, since the trap is undersized and the bait may not match what a rat is drawn to in the first place. Rodent Inspection and Estimates exists specifically to confirm which species is present before any treatment plan gets built, which saves the wasted effort of treating for the wrong animal.

What a confirmed identification changes.

Once the species is confirmed, the plan differs meaningfully. Mouse Control uses smaller traps and focuses on wall voids and kitchen areas, while Rat Control uses larger traps placed along rafters and roofline access points, often paired with a recommendation to trim back any tree limb providing a bridge onto the house.

The sound in your ceiling is already telling you something. Listen closely.

When it might be something else entirely.

Squirrels are a common source of daytime attic noise mistaken for rodents, since squirrels are active during daylight hours rather than at night, which is actually one of the easiest ways to rule them out. Raccoons produce a heavier, more deliberate sound and often enter through a larger opening than a rodent would need. If the noise happens consistently during the day rather than at night, mention that on the call, since it changes what we expect to find.

What to do while you wait for a visit.

Avoid the temptation to go into the attic yourself to investigate droppings or nesting material up close, since public health guidance recommends against disturbing dry droppings without wetting them first, and a rat nest can be more defensive than expected if it feels cornered. Noting the time the sound typically occurs and roughly which part of the attic it seems to come from gives the technician a useful starting point without requiring you to get any closer than necessary.

How long identification takes.

In most cases, a technician can confirm which animal is present within the first several minutes of an attic inspection, based on the combination of droppings, gnaw marks, grease trails, and the specific entry point found. A more ambiguous case, where signs of both species might be present, sometimes takes a bit longer to sort out, particularly on a property with more than one roofline or addition.

A note about the weather that night, and whether recent yard work or tree trimming happened nearby, can also help a technician connect the timing of new activity to a specific change on the property rather than treating it as an unrelated coincidence.

Why an accurate identification saves money.

Buying a store-bought trap kit built for the wrong species is a common, avoidable expense, since a mouse-sized snap trap does little against a rat and rat bait stations are unnecessarily large and costly for a mouse-only problem. Confirming the species first, whether through your own observation or a professional inspection, means any money spent on traps or exclusion material actually goes toward solving the problem in front of you rather than guessing at the wrong scale entirely.

What Mount Dora residents tell us most often.

Calls describing attic noise from this part of Lake County mention roof rats slightly more often than mice specifically, which tracks with how much of the city sits under mature tree canopy close to rooflines. That said, both remain common enough that neither should be assumed without at least some supporting evidence, whether that is droppings, gnaw marks, or a confirmed entry point.

Whichever animal turns out to be responsible, waiting rarely improves the situation. A small, contained problem caught early is a simpler and shorter visit than one that has had months to spread through an attic, a wall, or a yard.

Common questions

Can I really tell mice from rats just by the sound?

Sound alone is a useful clue but not a certain diagnosis. Light, quick movement suggests mice, while heavier, more localized sound suggests rats, but an inspection confirms it for certain.

Why does the sound happen at the same time every night?

An established nest often follows a routine, with the animal becoming active shortly after the house quiets down, which is a sign of settled activity rather than an animal just passing through.

Does tree cover near my house really matter?

Yes. Roof rats use tree limbs and power lines as bridges onto a roofline, so branches touching or close to the roof meaningfully raise the odds of a roof rat problem specifically.

What if the noise happens during the day instead of at night?

Daytime attic noise more often points to a squirrel than a rodent, since squirrels are active during daylight hours rather than at night.

Should I try to look in the attic myself first?

A quick, cautious look from the access point is fine, but avoid getting close to droppings or a suspected nest, and let a technician handle the rest of the inspection.
