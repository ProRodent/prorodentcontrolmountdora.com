Getting started.

Tell us the type of business, roughly how large the space is, and whether this is a first-time call or an ongoing concern. Restaurants and businesses that handle food have different priorities than an office or retail shop, particularly around delivery areas and dumpster placement, so knowing the business type helps plan the visit before arriving.

What a first commercial visit covers.

A first visit walks the entire property, inside and out, checking storage areas, kitchen equipment lines if the business handles food, utility rooms, the roofline, and any shared walls with a neighboring unit. The goal is a full baseline rather than a spot check of wherever activity happened to be noticed, since a business open to the public benefits from knowing the whole picture rather than treating one symptom at a time.

Why commercial properties are different.

A home deals with one household's routine, but a commercial property has deliveries, dumpsters, shared walls with neighboring businesses, and public hours that all affect where rodents find opportunity. A restaurant's dumpster enclosure, if not sealed and cleaned regularly, is one of the most reliable food sources a rat population can find anywhere in the city, and it draws activity toward the building itself over time.

Working around business hours.

Visits can usually be scheduled before opening, after closing, or during a slower part of the day depending on the business, so routine monitoring does not have to interrupt service to customers. For a restaurant, we typically plan around prep hours rather than the middle of service, and for a retail shop we work around whatever window is quietest for foot traffic.

Monitoring versus a single visit.

Many commercial accounts need a recurring monitoring schedule rather than a one-time treatment, both because ongoing foot traffic and food handling create continued opportunity and because some businesses need documentation of pest management for their own compliance records. A monitoring visit checks bait stations, looks for new activity, and updates records each time, building a history of the property's status rather than a single snapshot.

Retail and office considerations.

A retail shop without significant food handling still deals with stockroom storage, seasonal inventory sitting untouched for months, and shared walls with neighboring tenants that can carry activity between units. Offices see less pressure overall but are not immune, particularly a ground-floor space with a breakroom kitchen or a building with an aging roofline that has not been checked in years.

Downtown businesses and festival season.

Businesses along Donnelly Street and the surrounding blocks see a real spike in foot traffic and outdoor dining during any of Mount Dora's more than thirty annual festivals, and that spike means more food waste generated in a shorter window than a normal week. We recommend businesses in this area consider a tighter monitoring schedule around major festival dates rather than waiting for the standard visit to catch anything unusual.

Documentation for compliance.

A written record of each visit, what was checked, what was found, and what action was taken, supports a business that needs to show due diligence for a health inspection or an insurance requirement. We keep this documentation consistent from visit to visit so a business has a clear history rather than scattered notes.

A dumpster enclosure left unsealed undoes everything else.

Exclusion and repair for commercial buildings.

Rodent Exclusion and Entry Point Sealing for a commercial building often involves loading dock doors, shared utility chases with a neighboring unit, and larger rooflines than a typical house, all of which need the same careful sealing approach scaled to a bigger structure. Where damage has already occurred, Rodent Damage Repair covers ductwork, insulation, and structural fixes the same way it does for a residential property.

Working with property managers.

Buildings managed by a property management company rather than an individual owner sometimes need coordination between the manager, the tenant, and us, particularly when exclusion work involves a shared roofline or exterior wall. We are glad to work directly with a property manager on scheduling and documentation so a tenant is not stuck coordinating something outside their own lease responsibilities.

Seasonal businesses that scale up staffing and hours around festival weekends sometimes benefit from a short-term increase in monitoring frequency during that stretch rather than a permanent change to the regular schedule. We are happy to adjust a plan around a business's actual calendar instead of applying the same fixed interval regardless of how busy a particular month turns out to be.

Setting up an ongoing plan.

A new commercial account typically starts with a full inspection to establish a baseline, followed by a recommended monitoring interval based on what was found and the type of business involved. A quiet office might need only quarterly checks, while a restaurant near heavy foot traffic often benefits from a monthly schedule, and we explain the reasoning behind whatever interval is recommended.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about commercial rodent control

Do you offer ongoing contracts for businesses?

Yes, most commercial accounts are set up on a recurring monitoring schedule rather than a single visit.

Can you provide documentation for a health inspection?

Yes, each visit is documented with what was checked, found, and treated, building a consistent record over time.

How does festival season affect commercial pest pressure?

Increased foot traffic and food waste during major festivals can raise activity near downtown businesses, so a tighter schedule around those dates is often worthwhile.

Do you handle shared buildings with multiple tenants?

Yes, and we recommend coordinating the schedule across units sharing walls or a waste area, since activity in one unit affects the others.

What is the first step for a new business account?
