What a mouse call in Mount Dora usually looks like.

Most calls start the same way. Someone hears movement after dark, in the wall behind a headboard or in the attic above a bedroom closet. By the time they call, there is usually a second sign already, a chewed cereal box, a greasy smear along a baseboard, or a smell that was not there the week before. Mount Dora's older housing stock, especially the bungalows and cottages near the Historic Downtown District, tends to have more of the small gaps mice use than newer construction farther from the lake. A house mouse only needs an opening about the width of a pencil to get inside, so a slightly gapped dryer vent or an old plumbing chase behind a cabinet is plenty.

On the call we ask where you hear the activity, what time of day it happens, and whether you have noticed droppings, gnaw marks, or a persistent musty smell in a closed room. That gives the technician a starting point before they even pull into the driveway. Rodent Inspection and Estimates covers the full walkthrough if you want the entry points mapped before any traps go down, but for most active mouse problems we go straight into trapping and sealing on the same visit.

Finding where mice are moving.

A technician traces the route rather than guessing at it. That means checking the attic access point, the space behind the water heater, the gap under a sink cabinet, and anywhere pipes or wiring pass through an exterior wall. In homes near the lakefront, we also check where the roofline meets a chimney or dormer, since mice will use overhanging branches as a bridge onto the roof and then work their way down through a soffit gap. Kitchens and pantries get particular attention, since food access is usually what keeps a mouse population going once it gets inside.

Droppings tell a story on their own. Fresh droppings are dark and moist looking, while old ones turn gray and crumble easily, and the difference helps a technician tell whether a problem is new or has been building for a while. Gnaw marks on stored boxes, insulation pulled into a corner, or a faint ammonia smell near a wall cavity are all treated as active signs rather than something to note and move past.

Trapping and removing the mice already inside.

Traps go where the droppings and gnaw marks actually are, not in a generic pattern around the house. Snap traps and enclosed bait stations are placed along walls, behind appliances, and inside attic runs where mice travel, since mice tend to hug edges rather than cross open floor space. We check placements methodically over the visit and the follow-up, moving traps that are not producing results and adding more where activity is heaviest.

Every household is different, so tell the technician about any children or pets in the home before the visit starts. Bait stations are placed out of reach and explained clearly, and if a pet has access to a crawl space or garage, that changes where certain materials get placed.

Sealing the entry points for good.

Removing the mice already inside only buys time if the opening they used stays open. Rodent Exclusion and Entry Point Sealing uses steel wool, hardware cloth, and exterior-grade sealant matched to the material around the gap, since mice can chew through standard caulk and soft foam without much trouble. Vents, chimney chases, gaps around utility lines, and the underside of a garage door are the most common points we close on a typical Mount Dora visit.

Removal without sealing only resets the clock.

Homes with additions built at a different time than the original structure often have a seam where the two rooflines or foundations meet, and that seam is worth checking even if it looks tight from the ground. We point these out during the visit so you know what was found and what was closed.

Seasonal timing in Mount Dora.

Mount Dora does not get a hard freeze most winters, but the drop in overnight temperatures from November through February is still enough to push mice toward shelter. A warm attic or wall cavity next to a kitchen looks just as good to a mouse as an outdoor burrow. Calls tend to pick up in that stretch, along with a second, smaller bump during the wettest part of summer when heavy rain floods low ground near drainage areas and pushes rodents toward higher, drier structures nearby.

After the visit.

A follow-up check confirms whether the traps are clear and whether the sealed points are holding. If activity continues in a different part of the house, that usually means a second, unrelated entry point, which is common in a house with more than one addition or roofline. We would rather come back and confirm the job is finished than leave it uncertain.

Questions about mouse control

How can I tell if it is mice and not something bigger?

Mouse droppings are small, about the size of a grain of rice, while rat droppings are noticeably larger. Scratching that sounds light and quick, closer to scurrying than thumping, also points toward mice.

How do mice get into homes in Mount Dora?

A house mouse can squeeze through a gap about a quarter inch wide, so dryer vents, plumbing chases, and gaps where a roofline meets a chimney or addition are common points of entry, especially in the city's older housing stock.

Will traps alone solve the problem?

Trapping removes the mice that are already inside, but sealing the entry point is what keeps new ones from arriving. We treat both as part of the same visit whenever possible.

Is the treatment safe around children and pets?

Tell the technician about every child and pet in the home before the visit. Bait stations are placed out of reach and explained clearly, and placement changes if a pet has access to a garage or crawl space.

How quickly can someone come out?

Our line is answered day and night, and same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows.

What happens if mice come back after the visit?

We return to check the traps and confirm the sealed points are holding. New activity in a different part of the house usually points to a separate opening, which we track down on the follow-up.

Mouse calls come in from the older bungalows near the Historic Downtown District as often as they do from newer homes out toward Lakeside. Tell us the street and where you hear the scratching and we plan the visit from there.
